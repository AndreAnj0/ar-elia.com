5 signs your bed linen
is wearing out
faster than it should

A sheet rarely fails overnight. It degrades in stages — and nearly always because of the process, not the fabric. These are the five signs that show up first, what each one tells you about your wash, and how to confirm it before assuming the linen is to blame.

André Anjo 11 August 2026 7 min read

By the time housekeeping finally pulls a sheet out of circulation, the problem has been building for a while. Weeks pass between the moment an item stops being immaculate and the moment someone decides it will no longer do. In that gap, guests slept on it.

The good news is that textile degradation is not generic. Each kind of damage points to a specific cause in the process — water, chemical dosing, drying temperature, mechanical load. Reading the right sign avoids the most expensive decision of all: replacing a whole stock of linen when the problem was upstream and will destroy the new linen in exactly the same way.

01
Sign 01 · Appearance

The white that stopped being white

This is the first to appear and the easiest to ignore, because it happens slowly and the eye adjusts. But there is one distinction that changes the diagnosis completely: grey is not the same as yellow.

Um tom cinzento e uniforme across the whole item usually points to redeposition — soil that leaves the textile during the wash and settles back onto it, typically through insufficient rinsing, overloading the machine or hard water. Calcium and magnesium salts in the water combine with the surfactants and deposit in the fibre.

Um tom yellowish, especially if it sharpens after the calender, tells a different story: body fat that was poorly removed and oxidises under heat, residual alkalinity that was never neutralised, or iron in the water. The heat of the calender does not create the problem — it reveals it.

02
Sign 02 · Strength

Tears always in the same place

If the tears are scattered across the surface, the cause is probably chemical or general abrasion. If they appear concentrados nas bainhas, nos cantos ou ao longo da mesma linha de dobra, the problem is mechanical or in the drying.

The least suspected cause, and one of the most common, is over-drying. Cotton needs to hold some natural moisture. When the dryer runs on a fixed time instead of stopping on residual humidity, the lighter items in the load dry far beyond what is needed, the fibre turns brittle and breaks exactly where it flexes most: the hem.

It is also worth checking the obvious: overloading the machines reduces useful mechanical action and increases friction between items, and poorly protected bed-base or mattress corners always tear in the same spot.

03
Sign 03 · Handle

The handle that went hard

A sheet that has lost softness without losing its appearance is an early and very useful sign, because it shows up before any visible damage. In most cases it points to hard water: the salts build up in the fibre wash after wash and the textile stiffens cumulatively.

The other common cause is the absence or inadequacy of the neutralisation step. Industrial washing works in an alkaline medium; if the pH is not corrected in the final rinse, alkalinity is left in the item. That hardens the handle, contributes to yellowing under heat and can irritate a guest’s skin.

Watch for one trap: increasing the softener masks the symptom for a few washes, but it removes no deposits and corrects no pH. If softener consumption has been creeping up just to hold the same handle, that é the diagnosis.

04
Sign 04 · Chemicals

Small, isolated holes with clean edges

This is the most specific of the five signs, and the easiest to tell apart once you know what to look for. A hole caused by desgaste has frayed edges, with loose threads around it. A hole caused by chemical attack has clean edges, almost cut, and appears on its own in an item that is otherwise in good condition.

Clean-edged holes nearly always mean contact with concentrated product before it was diluted — bleach or an oxidising agent dosed directly onto dry textile, splashes during handling, or acid residue. Cotton is cellulose: it tolerates an alkaline medium well, but oxidisers and acids degrade it locally and the item tears at that point in the next wash.

If you find this pattern, the place to look is not the textile — it is the dosing system and the loading procedure.

05
Sign 05 · Dimensions

It no longer fits the mattress

A sheet can leave service with the fabric intact, simply because it no longer fits. That is a dimensional failure, not a wear failure, and it slips past the records because nobody logs it as a defect — it gets logged as «it doesn’t fit any more».

Some shrinkage in the first washes is normal and a serious supplier already allows for it in the measurements. What is not normal is for the item to keep shrinking throughout its service life. When that happens, the prime suspect is a systematically high drying temperature.

The cost here is silent: you are replacing serviceable linen, and the replacement rate shows up in the accounts with nobody able to explain why.

06
Method

The reference test

All of these signs share the same enemy: gradualness. The change is slow enough for the eye to adapt, which is why comparing from memory does not work.

The method is simple and costs nothing. From every batch of linen that comes in, keep one item unwashed, bagged and labelled with the date. Every three months, set it side by side with an item from the same batch that has been in circulation, in daylight, on a white surface. The difference you cannot see over time becomes obvious in direct comparison.

Before you buy new linen

Four of the six signs in the table point to the process, not the textile. If the cause lies in the water, the dosing or the drying, new linen will degrade in exactly the same way — only starting from scratch again, with the invoice already paid.

This is why the conversation about linen is rarely about linen. A good-quality item treated with a badly calibrated process lasts less than a mediocre item treated correctly — and the gap between the two extremes of a hotel sheet’s service life is not marginal.
