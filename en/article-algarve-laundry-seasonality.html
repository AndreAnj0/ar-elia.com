The hidden cost of
Algarve seasonality

Algarve hotels log 5.6 times more overnight stays per day in August than in January. We took the Statistics Portugal figures, applied them to a 50-room hotel and worked out what each way of handling the peak costs.

André Anjo 16 November 2026 Reading time: 8 min

Everyone who works in the Algarve knows August is not January. What matters to a laundry is by how much, and what that does to the sizing decision, which is made once and paid for over ten years.

This article uses Statistics Portugal’s monthly figures for Algarve hotels and applies them to one specific hotel. To run the same sums with your own property’s numbers, use the peak season calculator, which rests on the same data.

01
The data

What the statistics say about the year

Statistics Portugal (INE) publishes overnight stays in hotel establishments for each region every month (indicator 0012088). For the Algarve in 2025, three numbers sum up the year:

  • August had 5.6 times January’s overnight stays per day, and 3.7 times February’s.
  • From June to September, stays per day ran 55% above the yearly average. August alone ran 81% above. You often hear that summer brings an extra 30%; the data say almost double that.
  • 2024 was almost identical: 5.5 times between August and January. It is not an unusual year, it is the shape of the Algarve.
What these numbers do not say

These are stays for the whole region. Part of the winter drop is hotels closing, not hotels sitting empty, and a hotel open all year feels less than 5.6 times. So in the sums below we use the statistics’ shape of the year, month by month, but the amplitude of one specific hotel: its occupancy in the weakest month and in August.

02
The multiplier

Why linen rises faster than occupancy

A hotel that goes from 40% occupancy in January to 95% in August has 2.4 times as many occupied rooms. Linen volume rises further than that, for three reasons that stack:

  • More linen per occupied room. Summer brings pool and beach towels, which in winter simply do not exist.
  • More guests per room. In summer rooms fill with families: extra beds and more bath towels per occupied room.
  • The other textiles follow. A full restaurant, an open terrace, more uniforms in rotation.

In the sums that follow, that comes in as 5 kg of linen per occupied room in winter and 6.5 kg in August.

Assumptions: 50 rooms, 40% occupancy in January and 95% in August, 5 kg of linen per occupied room in winter and 6.5 kg in August. The months in between follow the statistics’ shape: if the region is halfway between January and August, so is the hotel.

The peak is 309 kg a day in August. The yearly average is 189 kg. January sits at 100 kg. Between the best and worst month, three times; between the peak and the average, 1.6 times.

Sized for August, this laundry uses 61% of its capacity on average over the year, and from November to February it runs below 40%. That capacity was paid for, and it depreciates, takes up space and needs maintenance anyway.

04
The options

Four capacities, and what each one costs

For the same hotel, here is what happens with four sizes of in-house laundry. Linen that does not fit goes out at €1.30 a kilo, the same price all year. What changes from size to size is idle capacity: equipment paid for and not used. We count €800 of equipment per kg/day of capacity (the €120,000 for 150 kg a day in the article on what an in-house laundry costs), depreciated over 10 years, plus 4% a year for maintenance. That comes to €112 per kg/day, per year.

Read top to bottom, the table is a trade: each step of capacity takes linen from outside and puts idle capacity inside. What matters is the price of each step, per kilo you stop sending out:

  • From the average to average +30%: €6,384 more a year to stop sending out 8,019 kg. That is €0.80 a kilo.
  • From average +30% to 90% of peak: €3,584 more a year for 1,984 kg. That is €1.81 a kilo, already above the €1.30 outside.
  • From 90% of peak to the peak: €3,472 more a year for the last 980 kg. That is €3.54 a kilo, almost three times washing them outside.

The last kilos of capacity are the most expensive a laundry ever buys. And these sums are equipment and maintenance only: washing in-house also takes staff, energy, water and chemicals, which sending out does not. Add those and every step only gets worse.

The 30% misleads here in another way. Anyone who sizes for the average plus 30% thinks they have covered summer. The data say summer is plus 55%, and what is left uncovered is exactly the July and August weeks when the whole region is looking for capacity at once. With the price the same all year, the risk in August is not paying more: it is finding nobody to wash it.

Outsource. Peak capacity becomes someone else’s problem. You pay per kilo, and an August kilo costs the same as a February one. It is what we do, and it is why this article exists, but the sums above do not depend on hiring us: they depend on someone having capacity when you need it.

Where these sums do not apply

Properties with steadier occupancy through the year, such as some of Faro’s urban hotels, have a different problem: the volume is steadier but small, and what blocks an in-house laundry is not the peak, it is the scale. Anyone who has run an in-house laundry for years, already written down and with their own staff, is in another position again. The figures above are for those deciding, not for those who decided long ago.

05
Calendar

The mistake of dealing with this in July

There is an asymmetry worth more than all the sums above: in August, the region’s capacity is committed. All of it. Anyone looking for a laundry then is looking in an emergency, with a broken calender or a volume they did not expect, and negotiates from the weakest position there is.

Winter is when this gets solved. It is when there is capacity to promise, when you can run a trial with real volume before there is any hurry, and when a supplier can plan its own high season counting on you.

Put more bluntly: the time to secure August is now.

06
In practice

What to ask before summer

Whether to your current supplier or to one you are assessing, these four questions quickly separate those who measure from those who improvise:

  • What is your daily capacity, and how much of it is committed in August? Anyone who cannot answer with a number has not planned.
  • Does the 24-hour turnaround hold in high season? And is it in writing, or an intention?
  • What happens if one of your machines breaks down in August? The answer shows whether there is a plan B or whether plan B is the client.
  • If my volume runs 40% above forecast, can you absorb it? How fast, and at what price.

None of these questions is about price per kilo. All of them are about what happens in the worst week of the year, which is the only time laundry becomes a management problem rather than a cost line.

07
Transparency

Where these numbers come from

The regional data are official. Everything else is a working assumption, set out here so you can argue with it:

  • The shape of the year — overnight stays in Algarve hotel establishments, month by month, in 2024 and 2025, from Statistics Portugal (INE). We compare stays per day, so that 28-day and 31-day months count equally. 2025 is the latest full year.
  • Occupancy of 40% in January and 95% in August — illustrative values for a coastal Algarve leisure hotel open all year. Your property has its own, and those are the ones the sums should use.
  • 5 kg of linen per occupied room — the assumption in our ROI calculator. 6.5 kg in August is our estimate, not a measurement, and varies with the type of property.
  • The months in between — each month sits between January and August in the same proportion as the region sits between January and August, for both occupancy and linen per room.
  • €1.30 a kilo washed outside, all year, and €800 of equipment per kg/day of capacity, depreciated over 10 years with 4% a year for maintenance — from the figures in the article on what an in-house laundry costs (€120,000 for 150 kg a day). We assume equipment cost grows in proportion to capacity; in practice you buy in machine-sized steps.

If any of these assumptions does not match your operation, the sums change. That is what the peak season calculator is for.

08
FAQ

The questions we get asked most

How much does an Algarve hotel’s linen volume rise in summer?

In Algarve hotels in 2025, August had 5.6 times January’s overnight stays per day, and from June to September stays per day ran 55% above the yearly average, according to Statistics Portugal. A hotel open all year sees less of a swing, because part of the regional drop is hotels closing, but between the weakest month and August linen volume easily reaches three times.

Why is the laundry peak bigger than the occupancy peak?

Because in summer each occupied room produces more linen: pool and beach towels come in, there are more guests per room, with families and extra beds, and restaurant and terrace textiles rise too. Occupancy can little more than double while linen volume triples.

Is it worth sizing an in-house laundry for the August peak?

For a 50-room hotel with the Algarve’s shape of year, sizing for August leaves 39% of capacity idle on average, around 13,400 euros a year of equipment and maintenance going unused. The last kilos of capacity are the most expensive: going from 90% of peak to the peak costs 3.54 euros for each kilo you stop sending out, almost three times washing it outside at 1.30 euros.

When should high-season capacity be negotiated?

In winter. In August the region’s capacity is committed, and anyone looking for a laundry then is looking in an emergency, which is the weakest negotiating position there is.
